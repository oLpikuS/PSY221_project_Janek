Hypothesis 1

Points allocated to a partner’s physical attractiveness differ by sex at birth.

Variables and descriptive statistics

  • Sex at birth (Sex_birth): A categorical variable with three groups: Male, Female, and Intersex.
  • Importance of partner physical attractiveness (preference_imp_physat): Points allocated to physical attractiveness from a total budget of 30 points across six partner characteristics. Higher values indicate greater relative importance.

Observed trend

Male respondents allocated more points to physical attractiveness on average than female respondents, although the distributions overlap substantially. The Intersex group was much smaller, so its distribution should be interpreted cautiously. These are descriptive patterns, not a test of the hypothesis.

H2. Confidence in finding a new partner and commitment

Confidence in finding a new partner is associated with relationship commitment.

Variables and descriptive statistics

  • Confidence in finding a new partner (qoa): A single item about finding another appealing partner if the current relationship ended. Responses range from 1 (Strongly disagree) to 7 (Strongly agree).
  • Relationship commitment (commitment): The mean of available responses to prqc_4, prqc_5, and prqc_6. Each item ranges from 1 (Not at all) to 7 (Extremely); higher scores indicate greater commitment.

This dataset includes respondents who were dating, in a committed relationship, or married.

Figure 2 shows the relationship between confidence and commitment. Points are jittered to make overlapping responses easier to see; the recorded scores are unchanged. The line summarizes the linear trend.

`geom_smooth()` using formula = 'y ~ x'

Observed trend

Code 
r <- cor(h2Data$qoa, h2Data$commitment, use = "complete.obs")
round(r, 2)
[1] -0.14

The correlation was -0.14, indicating a weak negative association. Respondents who reported more confidence in finding a new partner tended to report lower commitment.
